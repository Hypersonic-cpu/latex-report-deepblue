\chapter{Components}
\sectionlead{The body components are deliberately conservative: strong enough to make hierarchy obvious, but quiet enough for long technical documents.}

\section{Headings and hierarchy}
Chapter titles use navy with a thin pale-blue rule. Section headings use a small yellow square only as a locator; there are no large colored banners in the body. Subsection headings step down to a smaller blue sans-serif style.

\subsection{A representative subsection}
This is the default body level for most analysis. Equations remain conventional and inherit the surrounding typography:
\begin{equation}
  S = \frac{T_{\mathrm{baseline}}}{T_{\mathrm{proposed}}},
\end{equation}
where \(S\) is speedup, and the two terms denote the baseline and proposed execution times.

\subsection{Lists}
Lists use a small blue bullet and compact spacing. They work best when the items are genuinely parallel:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Keep section titles short and informational.
  \item Prefer full-width tables for methodology, prior work, and experiment matrices.
  \item Reserve yellow for small accents rather than body backgrounds.
\end{itemize}
